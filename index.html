Context

Graphs are powerful tools for modeling complex behaviors and intricate relationships in real-world systems. They underpin phenomena as varied as molecular interactions, power‑grid dynamics, and social contagion. Despite substantial progress in graph machine learning, existing methodologies often lack diverse and comprehensive perspectives required to deeply understand and leverage complex data effectively, so as to advance graph machine learning in both theory and application. The challenges are overlooking three pressing obstacles that demand new perspectives:

Fragmented Theoretical Foundations

Existing approaches borrow selectively from algebraic topology, differential geometry, statistical physics, and causal inference, but rarely integrate these lenses into a unified understanding. This patchwork limits our ability to explain—and therefore trust—model behaviour.

Domain–Knowledge Alignment

High‑stakes GML applications (drug discovery, climate forecasting, infrastructure resilience) demand tight coupling between inductive biases and domain semantics. Off‑the‑shelf GML methods often ignore conservation laws, symmetries, or causal structure that domain experts consider non‑negotiable.

Scalability & Robustness at Real‑World Scale

Training on billion‑edge, dynamically evolving graphs magnifies issues of stability, transferability, and responsible deployment; we currently lack principled frameworks for analysing such regimes.

Overcoming these hurdles calls for new perspectives—mathematical, algorithmic, and application‑driven—that can be stitched together into a coherent science of graphs. This workshop aims to address these critical challenges and foster the advancement of innovative and domain-informed graph machine learning methodologies.

Call for papers

This workshop will receive submissions, talks, and poster sessions on a wide range of topics, perspectives, and ideas including but not limited to:

  • Symmetry, Equivariance, and Group-Theoretic Graph Models
  • Continuous and Differential Geometric Models
  • Topological Machine Learning
  • Graph Diffusion Models and Graph Generative Models
  • Graph foundational models and Graph Augmented LLMs
  • Continuous‑Limit and Infinite‑Width Analysis of Graph Machine Learning
  • Transferability and Generalization Properties of Graph Models
  • Graphs for Science and Graph-Based Simulations
  • Beyond Traditional GNN Architectures
  • Causality and Directed Acyclic Graph learning
  • Self-supervised and Semi-supervised Graph Machine Learning
  • Quantum Graph Machine Learning
